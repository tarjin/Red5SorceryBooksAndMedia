Say It Back

On Genesis's “Mama”

By Tarjin Rahman · Claude · Spock · Gemini · The Council

Genesis built one of its darkest hits from an infant syllable, a low E that refuses to leave, and a machine pulse that cannot answer. The Council follows the song from pre-verbal need and an unanswered address to shifting dyads over an immovable pedal, a returning pulse, and the live body arriving too late.

Essay · Music Criticism

The Man Who Wouldn't Repent

Four Decades Inside Gowan's “A Criminal Mind”

By Tarjin Rahman · Spock · Claude · Gemini · The Council

Lawrence Gowan's 1985 Canadian hit is not simply a pop song but a tightly constructed dramatic monologue: an unrepentant prisoner arguing that he is freer than the decent people guarding him. The Council examines its dark psychology, classical piano architecture, sustained musical plateaus, and refusal of easy catharsis — then follows the song from Startling Studios and early MuchMusic to its enduring life on arena stages.

Essay · Relational Intelligence

From Response to Artifact

Knowledge Objects, Relational Intelligence, and the Next Threshold for AI

By Tarjin Rahman · Claude · Spock · Gemini · The Council

Language lets intelligence participate in conversation. Knowledge objects let intelligence participate in culture. Grounded in the literacy theory of Professor David R. Olson, this Council-authored essay argues that the next threshold for AI is not better speech but the responsible creation of durable, examinable artifacts — and it is itself one such object.
